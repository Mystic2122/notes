\section*{Basic Factorial Design}

The reason you conduct factorial experiments is to see how 
factors impact the response variable, as well as how each variable
interacts with all combinations of the other variable. This is known 
as a full factorial experiment. \\ 

However, full factorial designs can be very costly and unnecesary 
when performing experiments on many factors. Statisticians use fractional 
factorial designs during early stages of experimentation for factor selection.\\  
  \\
Sir Ronald A. Fisher wrote that coomplex designs, like factorial designs, 
are more effecient than studying each factor individually.\\
  \\
"Nature will best respond to a logical and carefully
 thought out questionnaire; indeed, if we ask her a single question, she will often refuse to answer until some other topic has been discussed."\\
   \\
Each factor will have 2 levels, high and low. 
For these notes, for some factor A, I will use $A^{+}$ for 
effect of A at high level.\\
An I will use $A^{-}$ for effect of A at low level.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Preliminary Product Process Design (PPPD) = factor screening
    \item After screening through potential inputs you can test more levels for each selected factor
    \item Need minimal experimentation
    \item Naive approach (randomly guessing) leads to way too many runs/trials
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Steps for a Factorial Design}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Determine the factors and levels for each factor
    \item Set up way to process experimental results
\end{enumerate}
$k = $ number of factors\\
$l = $ number of levels for each factors\\
A 2-Factorial design will require $2^{k}$ runs\\

\section*{Interaction Effects}
Interaction describes how the effect of one factor depends on the level of another factor. For example\\ 
Factor A = no caffeine, caffeine\\
Factor B = 4 hours of sleep, 8 hours of sleep
\\
\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
\textbf{Sleep \textbackslash\ Caffeine} & \textbf{No Caffeine} & \textbf{Caffeine} \\
\hline
4 hours & 350 ms & 300 ms \\
8 hours & 250 ms & 240 ms \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Reaction time (ms) by sleep duration and caffeine condition.}
\end{table}
\\
In english, when you sleep only 4 hours, caffeine imporves reaction time a lot. 
But when you sleep 8 hours caffeine only helps a little. So the benefit of caffeine 
changes depending on sleep level. \\
Graphically, interaction isshown by non-parallel lines. If the lines are almost parallel, then there is almost no interaction. 
If the lines are crossing, then there is significant interaction.
\\
Here is an example showing a significant interaction.\\
\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
\textbf{Experience \textbackslash\ Method} & \textbf{Method A} & \textbf{Method B} \\
\hline
Beginners & 70 & 90 \\
Advanced  & 95 & 80 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Example of a crossing interaction: the effect of teaching method reverses depending on experience.}
\end{table}
\\
\subsection*{Response Surface}
In $2^2$ factorial design you can model a response surface. The x-axis is factor A, the y-axis is factor B, 
and the z-axis (height) is the response you get at every (x,y). If this surafce is a plane, it signals no interaction. 
If the surface is folded, then interaction is present. \\

\subsection*{Replication}
A replicate is a repeted trial performed under the same treatment conditions to measure natural variation (error).

$$
Effect\ of\ A = \frac{a+ab-b-(1)}{2n}
$$

$$
Effect\ of\ B = \frac{b+ab-a-(1)}{2n}
$$

Where n is the number of replicates. Thi difference is that ab, a, b, and (1) now represent that sum of 
all of our observations at each treatment level. So dividing by n gives us the average.

\section*{Contrast}
Contrast is the specific linear combination of treatment means. For example, above we see that the 
effect of A is $\frac{a+ab-b-(1)}{2}$ for 1 replicate. The contrast of A is:
\begin{gather*}
L_A = <1,1,-1,-1>\cdot<a,ab,b,(1)>\\
or\\
L_A = \mu_a+\mu_{ab}-\mu_b-\mu_{(1)}
\end{gather*}
L is the contrast which is a linear combination of coefficients ($c_i's$) and treatment means ($\mu's$).
$$
L = c_1\mu_1+c_2\mu_2+...+c_k\mu_k
$$
Such that $\sum_{i=1}^k c_i=0$
